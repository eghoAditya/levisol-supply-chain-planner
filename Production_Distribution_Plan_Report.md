**The plan meets every unit of customer demand.** Of the 8,109.7 kl of Jan-2026 demand, 2,973 kl is served straight from stock already sitting at the CFAs (Exhibit I opening inventory) and the remaining 5,136 kl is shipped fresh from the hubs. The only shortfall anywhere is 17.6 kl of *internal hub buffer stock* (1.0% of the safety-stock target) on production lines that are at full capacity — this does not affect any customer order, it only slightly thins the hubs' shock absorber for those specific SKUs.

> Note on production volume: 6,800 kl is produced but only ~5,929 kl is shipped to hubs. The ~871 kl difference is unavoidable 25 kl batch-rounding surplus (you cannot produce a fractional batch of a grade); it is held as plant closing inventory — a carried-forward asset, not waste. Pushing it downstream would cost freight for no service benefit, so the cost-minimising plan leaves it at the plant.

## 2. What We Are Optimising

**Objective:** minimise total operating cost = production cost + plant→hub freight + hub→CFA freight + penalty cost of any unmet demand + penalty cost of any hub safety-stock shortfall.

We price unmet demand rather than forbidding it, so the model always returns an actionable plan and makes the same economic trade-off a planner would. Contractual SKUs carry a 5× penalty multiplier so they are protected first when capacity is tight.

## 6. What We Chose Not to Supply, and Why

**No customer demand was sacrificed.** The only under-fulfilment is hub safety stock, concentrated on SKUs whose production lines are fully utilised:

- The 180–210 LT drum line and the ≤1.5 LT small-pack line are the binding capacity constraints.
- For 11 SKU×hub combinations, topping the hub buffer to 100% of target would require producing on a more expensive plant/line, and the freight+production cost of doing so exceeds the priced risk of a marginally thinner buffer.
- The model leaves these buffers 1–20% short rather than spend more — a defensible cash-vs-service trade-off, and exactly the "which cost are you willing to pay" judgement the brief asks for.

Because this is a soft, priced decision, a planner who disagrees can raise the safety-stock penalty weight in the tool and the model will instead protect the buffer (at higher cost). The plan is a recommendation, not a black box.
